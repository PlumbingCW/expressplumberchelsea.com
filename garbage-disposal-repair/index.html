Garbage disposal repair in Chelsea, MA covers units that hum but will not spin, trip the breaker, leak from the base or flange, drain slowly or smell. Most problems come from a jam, a worn seal or a clogged drain beyond the unit.

Never put your hand in a disposal. Turn the power off at the switch and breaker before any work. If water is leaking under the sink, close the supply valves and call us.

Humming, jammed or dead: quick checks

A humming disposal that does not spin is jammed. Turn the power off, use the hex socket at the bottom to turn it back and forth, then press the reset button. A silent disposal may have tripped its reset or the circuit.

If the unit works but water backs up in the sink, the clog is often in the trap or line past the disposal, which our drain cleaning service handles.

What not to grind

Grease, coffee grounds, eggshells, fibrous vegetables such as celery and potato peels, and pasta or rice that swells all cause clogs. Grease is the worst, since it coats the drain and hardens farther down.

Chelsea's combined sewer system adds to the case against pouring grease down any drain, because fats build up in the city line and increase backup risk during storms.

Safe habits around a disposal

Keep utensils and hands out of the chamber, and use tongs or pliers if something drops in. Turn the power off at the switch and the breaker before you reach for anything. Never use a screwdriver or a chemical to free a jam.

Run cold water while the unit is on and for a short time afterward. Cold water keeps fats solid so they are chopped and flushed, instead of melting and coating the pipe.

Dishwashers and disposals together

A dishwasher drain hose often connects to the disposal. If it clogs, water from the dishwasher can back up into the sink or leak. A high loop in the hose or an air gap prevents backflow, and we check it during a visit.

If the dishwasher leaves standing water after a cycle, the disposal inlet may still have its knockout plug in place. That is a quick fix.

Choosing a replacement disposal

Horsepower, feed type and noise level matter. A continuous-feed unit handles daily family use, and a batch-feed model adds safety around children. Check that the new unit fits the existing sink flange and the cabinet space.

A new disposal needs power, a dishwasher drain connection and a knockout plug removed if a dishwasher is present. We handle each of those steps.
