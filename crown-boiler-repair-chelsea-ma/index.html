Crown Boiler Repair in Chelsea, MA: Common Faults and Fixes

    Talk to a plumber
    (617) 752-0573
    Call Now

    Written by the Chelsea Express Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16. Updated 2026-11-16.

    Crown is one of the boiler brands found in older New England homes, and many of those boilers have been running for decades. Age brings predictable problems, and most are fixable if you catch them early.

    We service many boiler makes, including Crown units, and this guide covers the faults we hear about most, what you can check yourself and when to call for service.

    No heat: what to check first

    Check the thermostat, the boiler switch and the breaker. Look at the pressure gauge. A cold hot-water system normally sits around 12 psi, and a reading near zero means a leak or a failed valve.

    If you smell gas or hear a carbon monoxide alarm, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility. Do not try to relight the burner if you are unsure.

    Steam or hot water: know your system

    Steam boilers heat water to make steam that rises through radiators. They need the correct water level, working vents and sloped pipes. Hot water boilers pump heated water through baseboards or radiators and use an expansion tank.

    The repair path differs. A steam system problem is often a vent or low-water cutoff, while a hot water problem is more often a pump, valve or air. Our boiler repair page explains the process.

    Repair or replace an older boiler

    A well-kept boiler can run for decades, but an old cast iron section that leaks, or a boiler with repeated failures, may cost more to keep than to replace. Efficiency also matters, since older boilers burn more fuel for the same heat.

    We give you the repair price, the likely remaining life and the replacement price so you can decide with real numbers.

    Maintenance that prevents winter calls

    A yearly service cleans the burner, tests safety devices and checks the vent. Bleed radiators, check the pressure gauge in the fall and keep the area around the boiler clear.

    If your boiler also heats domestic hot water through a coil or indirect tank, a boiler failure can leave you without hot water as well. See water heater repair for related service.

    Chelsea local note

    Chelsea's older buildings often have boilers in basements shared among apartments, and winter cold snaps push those systems hard. Massachusetts requires gas work to be done by licensed professionals under the state gas code.

    MWRA water is soft, so scale is a smaller problem here than in hard water areas, though sediment still builds with age. Source types: state plumbing and gas licensing guidance and MWRA water quality information.

    Reading the boiler's age and nameplate

    The nameplate lists the model, the serial number and the input rating. The serial number often encodes the manufacture date, and a manufacturer or plumber can decode it. Knowing the age helps you decide whether a repair is worth the cost.

    Take a photo of the nameplate and keep it with your records. It saves time on the phone and helps with parts.

    Common parts that fail on older boilers

    Circulator pumps, thermostats, low-water cutoffs, aquastats and gas valves are the usual suspects. Each is a replaceable part, and many older boilers keep running for years with new components. A cracked cast iron section is different, and it usually means replacement.

    We test each control with a meter before we replace it. Guessing at parts wastes money and leaves the real problem in place.

    Fuel types and what changes

    Gas boilers need a clean burner, a working ignition and a clear vent. Oil boilers add a nozzle, a filter and a fuel pump, and they need regular cleaning for soot. Each has its own service routine, and a licensed technician for the correct fuel should do the work.

    If you are thinking about switching fuels, the cost depends on the gas line, the chimney and the building. Ask for a written plan before you commit.

    Before the first cold night: a short seasonal checklist

    Test the thermostat, bleed the radiators, check the pressure gauge and look for drips at valves and fittings. Clear the area around the boiler of boxes and laundry, and make sure the flue and any intake pipe are open. If the boiler has not run since spring, run it on a mild day to see how it behaves.

    Schedule a service visit before the first cold snap, not during it. Technicians are busiest on the coldest nights, and a calm visit finds problems that an emergency call would not.
