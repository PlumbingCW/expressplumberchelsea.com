Sewer inspection in Chelsea, MA uses a waterproof camera on a flexible cable to travel through your drain and sewer line and record what it finds. Cracks, offset joints, roots, bellies, scale and breaks all show up on screen.

The inspection answers the question behind most sewer calls: is the line damaged or just dirty? That determines whether you need cleaning, a spot repair or a larger project.

When an inspection makes sense

Recurring backups, slow drains in several fixtures, gurgling toilets and sewage smells point to a main line problem. An inspection is also smart before buying an older building, after a clog that returns and before any major sewer repair.

Chelsea's older buildings often have clay, cast iron or Orangeburg pipe, and those materials fail in different ways. Seeing the actual pipe lets us recommend the right fix.

Why Chelsea's sewer system matters to your line

Roughly 70% of Chelsea's sewer system is combined, meaning stormwater and sewage share pipes. In heavy rain those pipes can surcharge, and a weak or low private lateral is more likely to back up into basements.

The camera shows whether your private line is part of the problem or whether the surge is coming from the street. The city's ongoing separation work reduces flows over time, but it does not repair a cracked lateral.

How an inspection works

  1. Find accessWe use a cleanout, vent or removed toilet to enter the line.
  2. Run the cameraThe camera travels the line while recording video and tracking distance.
  3. Locate defectsA locator marks the depth and position of problem spots on the surface.
  4. Explain and recommendWe review the footage with you and give you a clear next step.

Reading the findings with us

After the camera pass, we sit with you and replay the key moments. We point out the type of pipe, the joints, the defects and the distance from the cleanout. Ask questions, because the footage is yours to keep.

A defect is not always a problem. A small offset or a light scale may be fine for years, and we say so. If something needs action soon, we tell you why and what the options are.

Planning ahead for repairs or a sale

If the footage shows trouble, a written scope for lining or replacement lets you compare costs, schedule the work and talk to your insurer or the buyer. If it shows a healthy pipe, keep the recording for your records.

Many buyers ask for a sewer scope in their inspection period. Having a recent recording speeds a sale and reduces negotiation about the line.

Real estate inspections

Buyers of older Chelsea two- and three-family homes often add a sewer scope to their inspection. A line that looks fine at the toilet can be cracked under the basement slab or at the street connection.

Sellers also use an inspection to disclose line condition and negotiate repairs before listing. We provide a written summary of what the camera found.
