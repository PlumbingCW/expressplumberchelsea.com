Sump pump repair in Chelsea, MA covers pumps that will not start, run constantly, make noise or fail to move water. Common causes include a stuck float switch, a clogged intake, a failed motor, a frozen or blocked discharge line and a power outage.

If water is rising in the pit and the pump is silent, check that it is plugged in and that the breaker has not tripped. If that does not help, call our 24/7 line, and move belongings off the floor.

Basement water and Chelsea's coast

Chelsea sits on low ground beside the Mystic and Chelsea Creek, so basements near the waterfront see a high water table in storms and high tides. A pump has to cope with groundwater and, in heavy storms, additional water pushed by surface flooding.

Combined sewers add another risk. During intense rain the sewer system can back up. Many communities prohibit connecting a sump pit to the sanitary sewer, so ask the city and your plumbing inspector where yours may discharge.

Sump pump emergency: backup power and a second pump

Storms often take down power at the moment you need the pump most. A battery backup pump or a water-powered backup keeps the pit from overflowing during an outage.

A second pump set a little higher in the pit also covers the case where the main pump fails. We size the system to your basement and the pit.

How a sump pump repair goes

  1. Check power and floatWe test the circuit, outlet and float movement.
  2. Inspect the pit and dischargeWe look for debris, a blocked line and a working check valve.
  3. Repair or replaceWe service the pump or install a new one with a check valve.
  4. Test under loadWe fill the pit and watch the pump cycle and clear the water.

Preparing for storm season

Test the pump, clear the pit of grit and check that the discharge line is open. Plug the pump into a working outlet, and check the battery on any backup system. If a major storm is forecast, make sure stored items are off the floor.

Keep our number near the basement door. In a bad storm, calls pile up, and the sooner you reach dispatch, the sooner a plumber can plan the route.

When the pit itself is the problem

A pit that is too small, cracked or full of silt makes any pump work harder. We check the pit depth, the lid, the liner and the way water enters it. Sometimes a new pit or a second pit solves recurring failures.

If water enters through the floor slab in many places, a perimeter drain helps. See basement waterproofing for that approach.

Discharge lines and winter

A discharge pipe that ends against a wall or drains toward the foundation can recycle water back into the basement. In cold weather, an exposed line can freeze and block the flow.

We route the discharge away from the building and recommend a freeze-resistant end. For a larger water problem, a pump may be only part of the fix.
