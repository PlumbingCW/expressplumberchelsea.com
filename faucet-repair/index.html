Faucet Repair and Replacement in Chelsea, MA

A drip wastes more water than it looks like, and a leaking handle can rot a vanity from the inside. Most faucet problems are small and fixable.

Faucet repair in Chelsea, MA fixes drips from the spout, leaks around the handle and base, low flow, loose handles and faucets that will not shut off. The cause is usually a worn cartridge, washer, O-ring or clogged aerator.

If a faucet will not shut off and is flooding the sink, close the small valves under it. If those are stuck or missing, turn off the main and call us.

Why faucets drip and leak

Inside a faucet, rubber seals and cartridges wear with use. A drip at the spout means the seal no longer closes, while a leak at the handle or base means an O-ring or packing has failed.

Low flow usually points to a clogged aerator or a partly closed supply valve. Mineral deposits are less of an issue in Chelsea because MWRA water is soft, but sediment can still gather in older fixtures.

Outdoor spigots and winter risk

In Chelsea's cold months an outdoor faucet that still has a hose attached can freeze and split inside the wall. The damage often shows only when you turn it on in spring and water sprays inside.

Disconnect hoses in the fall and close indoor shutoffs where your building has them. A frost-free sillcock is a better long-term fix, and we can install one. A burst inside the wall may need emergency pipe repair.

What a faucet repair includes

  1. Identify the fixtureWe find the brand and cartridge type or recommend replacement if parts are gone.

  3. Replace worn partsCartridges, seals, aerators or the entire faucet.
  4. Test for leaksWe run hot and cold, check under the sink and confirm tight connections.

What a faucet repair tells you about the rest of the plumbing

A faucet that sputters or runs rusty can point to a problem upstream, such as a failing shutoff valve, a corroded supply line or a pressure issue. We check what feeds the faucet while we have the sink open, so a small repair does not hide a larger issue.

Old shutoff valves under sinks often fail when they are turned. If yours will not close or drips when moved, replacing it is a good idea at the same visit.

Choosing a replacement faucet

Look at the number of holes in the sink or counter, the reach of the spout and how the handle feels. Single handle faucets are easy to use, while two-handle designs suit some older sinks. Pull-down sprayers help with large pots.

Choose a finish that is easy to keep clean in soft water. Chelsea's MWRA supply does not leave heavy white scale, so most finishes stay clear with a quick wipe.

Repair or replace the faucet

A newer faucet with a worn cartridge is cheap to repair. An old, corroded faucet with no available parts costs more to keep fixing than to replace.

If you are updating a kitchen or bath, we install new faucets, supply lines and shutoff valves, and we can add them to a larger fixture installation visit.
