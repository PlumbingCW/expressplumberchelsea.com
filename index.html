Around Chelsea

Neighborhoods and what we see in them

Chelsea is small but varied. Each neighborhood has its own building stock and its own usual plumbing problem.

Bellingham Square

The historic central square near City Hall. Mixed-use buildings here share drains between shops and apartments, so a kitchen backup can reach the units above and below.

Box District and Prattville

Dense blocks of older multi-family houses. Original cast iron stacks and galvanized supply lines are the usual cause of slow drains and low pressure.

Mill Hill and Addison-Orange

Residential streets with older two- and three-family houses. Water heaters in small basement closets fail here more often than any other fixture.

Admirals Hill and the Naval Hospital Historic District

Historic buildings with older piping and boilers. Heating and hot water calls rise sharply in cold snaps.

Waterfront District along Chelsea Creek

Low ground close to tidal water, where sump pumps, floor drains and basement dampness matter most after storms.

Soldiers' Home and the Broadway corridor

Larger institutional and commercial buildings that need dependable restroom, hot water and drain service.

Chelsea sits beside the Mystic River and Chelsea Creek, and the Chelsea Street Bridge connects it to East Boston. That waterfront setting is why basement water and storm backups come up so often.
