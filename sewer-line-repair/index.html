Sewer line repair in Chelsea, MA fixes a broken, offset, rooted or collapsed pipe between a building and the street connection. Cleaning opens the line for a while, but a damaged pipe keeps failing until the damaged section is repaired or relined.

If sewage is backing into a basement, treat it as an emergency, stop using water, and call our 24/7 line. A plumber can clear the line first and then show you on camera what the real problem is.

Sewer backup emergency: what to do first

Stop running water, keep out of wet areas, and do not touch electrical equipment in a flooded basement. Take photographs for the claim and open a window if you can do it safely.

Tell dispatch whether the backup started after heavy rain or on a dry day. A dry-day backup points toward a damaged or blocked lateral on your property.

What the camera usually finds in older Chelsea pipe

Much of Chelsea was rebuilt after the fire of April 12, 1908, which burned about 350 acres. Buildings from that era may still have clay or cast iron laterals installed a century ago, so joints leak, roots enter and sections shift.

A camera shows the pipe material, how far the damage extends and whether the pipe can be saved. That answer decides between a short repair and a longer replacement, and it is the reason we scope before we quote.

Lining can be a good fit under a driveway or a finished basement floor because it avoids a long trench. Whether it is allowed and how it is permitted is a question for the local plumbing inspector, so we confirm that before any work begins.

How a repair job moves forward

  1. Stabilize the backupWe clear the line enough to stop the flooding and make the pipe readable for a camera.
  2. Map and diagnoseThe camera and a locator mark the depth and location of the failure on the surface.
  3. Present the optionsYou see the footage and get repair choices with the trade-offs for each, not just one price.
  4. Repair and testAfter the work, the line is run with water and inspected again on camera.

Cast iron pipe and older building drains

Cast iron rots from the inside out, so a pipe that looks fine from the basement can be heavily scaled within. If a camera shows thin walls and heavy scale, relining or replacement saves repeated emergency calls.

Inside the building, a cracked cast iron stack can leak into a wall. Odor with no visible water is a reason to have the stack inspected, and leak detection can pinpoint a drain-line leak without opening the whole wall.
