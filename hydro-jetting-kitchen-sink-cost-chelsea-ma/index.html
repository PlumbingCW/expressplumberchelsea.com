Hydro Jetting a Kitchen Sink Line in Chelsea, MA: What Drives the Cost

    Written by the Chelsea Express Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16. Updated 2026-11-16.

    Hydro jetting cleans a drain with high-pressure water instead of a cable. For kitchen lines coated in grease, it is often the better method, and it costs more than a basic snake visit. We do not publish a flat number, because the price depends on the line, and this guide explains the factors.

    You will see what makes a jetting job larger or smaller, how to decide if it is worth it and how to keep the line clean afterward.

    Why jetting costs more than snaking

    A cable tool pokes a hole through a clog. A jetter uses a nozzle that spins and pushes water backward along the pipe wall, which scrubs grease, scale and roots away. The equipment is larger and more expensive, and the technician needs more setup time.

    The result lasts longer when the problem is buildup instead of one obstruction. If the clog is a lost utensil or a mass of hair, a cable is the right tool and cheaper.

    Is jetting safe for old pipes?

    A camera inspection comes first. If the pipe is cracked, offset or paper-thin, high-pressure water can make the damage worse. In those cases, we recommend cleaning gently and repairing the line.

    Our hydro jetting service page explains the process. If the camera shows damage, see repair options.

    Keeping a kitchen line clean after jetting

    Scrape pans, keep grease out of the sink and run hot water for a minute after washing dishes. If you cook a lot, consider scheduling a yearly cleaning. Grease returns in a kitchen that is used daily.

    In multi-family buildings, the stack matters as much as your sink. We can jet the whole run so one household does not clog another.

    Chelsea local note

    Chelsea's sewer system is about 70 percent combined, so grease that leaves a kitchen line ends up in pipes shared with stormwater. The Water and Sewer Department asks residents and businesses not to pour fats, oils and grease down drains.

    For restaurants, grease control rules apply. Ask the city what is required for your property. Source types: city sewer information and Water and Sewer Department guidance.

    Questions to ask when you get a price

    Ask whether the price includes a camera before and after, how many passes the technician expects and what happens if the line turns out to be damaged. Ask what the pressure setting will be for your pipe material. A clear answer shows the contractor is planning the job instead of guessing.

    Ask for a written scope. It protects you and the plumber if the line turns out to be larger or more damaged than expected.

    What a cheaper visit may leave out

    A low price can mean a quick snake of the trap and nothing more. That is fine when the clog is in the trap, but it will not scrub a coated line. If the same sink clogs again within a few weeks, the first visit probably addressed the symptom only.

    We would rather tell you honestly that a cable is enough than sell a jetting visit you do not need.

    How often a kitchen line should be cleaned

    A busy household kitchen may do well with a cleaning every year or two. A restaurant may need monthly work. A little-used apartment kitchen may go years. The right interval comes from what the camera shows, not from a calendar.

    Landlords with several units can schedule jetting for the shared stack at the same visit, which reduces the cost per unit.

    When jetting is the wrong tool

    A jetter is not a cure-all. If a pipe is cracked, collapsed or has a major belly, jetting can spread water into the soil or fail to clear the line. If the clog is a single solid object, a cable or a retrieval tool is faster and cheaper.

    That is why we start with a camera and a conversation. A plumber who recommends the same method for every drain is guessing, and you pay for the guess.

    What to do before the plumber arrives

    Empty the cabinet under the sink so the technician can reach the trap and the cleanout. Stop running the sink and the dishwasher, and do not pour chemical cleaner in, because it is a hazard when water is jetted back up. Tell us if you have used one.

    Do not leave pets or children near the work area. If wastewater is already rising, tell dispatch right away so the plumber brings the right equipment.
