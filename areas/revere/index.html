Housing built before modern plumbing codes

Much of Revere was built out in the first half of the twentieth century, and many of its homes still carry the original cast iron or clay drain lines. These materials last a long time but crack, offset and collect roots, so sewer backups are a regular reason people call us.

A camera sewer inspection shows the real condition of the lateral, and from the footage we advise on cleaning, lining or a section replacement.

Revere neighborhoods and what we see in them

Revere Beach and Ocean Avenue

Condo towers and older beachfront buildings share risers and water heaters. Water heater failures in shared basements are common, and we coordinate with building management.

Beachmont and Shirley Avenue

Dense blocks of two- and three-family homes with older drain stacks. Plans for water heater replacement and drain work often overlap here.

Oak Island and Point of Pines

Low coastal ground where storms and high tides can push water into basements. A sump pump and a working check valve are worth checking before the next nor'easter.

Near Wonderland and Route 1A

Commercial and mixed-use buildings along the main roads call us for drains, grease lines and restrooms.

Why tides and storms matter to your plumbing

Revere has miles of shoreline and low neighborhoods near Mill Creek. During storms and king tides, the ground fills with water and basements feel it first. Pumps run harder, and any weak drain connection leaks.

Before storm season, test your pump, clear the discharge and make sure floor drains work. For basements that stay damp, our basement waterproofing page explains the options.

Water, hardness and your fixtures

Revere gets MWRA water, which is soft at about one grain per gallon. Scale is rarely the problem. Age, corrosion and movement in the building are the real causes of leaks.

That is good news for tankless units and dishwashers, and it means tankless service here is more about venting and gas lines than descaling.
