From box factories to a mixed-income neighborhood

The Box District sits about one block from Chelsea City Hall. It takes its name from the box-manufacturing companies that once filled it, the earliest being the Russell Box Company, which began operating in 1903 at the foot of Gerrish Avenue. After the factories left, the area was rezoned for housing in the 2000s.

The Urban Land Institute describes a 10.5-acre site redeveloped from 2005 onward. Box District Park opened in 2012, and the neighborhood has a Silver Line station and a linear path.

In a managed building, start with the manager

Most Box District homes are rented or sit inside a building with a manager. If a tap leaks or a toilet runs, tell your manager first, because the owner usually arranges the repair. That is the quickest path and it protects you if the problem is a building line.

If you own a townhome or manage a building, call us. We can help with leak detection, water heater repair and toilet repair, and we will say plainly what needs a visit.

For a building with several units, tell us how many floors share the line and whether anyone has reported the problem before. That history often points to the cause faster than the fixture itself.

Water and sewer facts for all of Chelsea

Two citywide facts apply to every building here. MWRA water is soft, near 1 grain per gallon, and roughly 70% of the city's sewers carry stormwater and waste in the same pipe. Neither is a Box District finding.
