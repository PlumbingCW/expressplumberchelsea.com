A hill with a long record

Powder Horn Hill is one of the earliest named hills in the Boston Basin. The city bought the top in 1897 as a public park, which is now Malone Park, and WPA workers leveled the hill for the park in 1937. The Soldiers Home was established on the hill in 1883, in an 1858 hotel, and brick Colonial Revival buildings followed around 1900.

City planning materials list the Soldiers Home as a civic node. The homes around it are a different matter. Public descriptions call the neighborhood one of the least dense in Chelsea, with steep slopes and Queen Anne style houses.

What the Historical Commission says about the homes

The 1980 Historical Commission report says Italianate center-entrance houses were built on the lower slopes, and that Colonial Revival and Craftsman two-family houses and triple deckers were added on the north side of the hill in the early 20th century.

That report is more than forty years old and describes styles, not pipes. We do not assume what is inside a house from its look. We check the actual lines, starting with a camera inspection when the symptoms point there.

Living on a slope

On a hillside lot, water runs downhill, so it is worth looking at where your downspouts, your sump discharge and your driveway drain send it. We do not claim anything about Powder Horn Hill drainage, and we have no data on it. This is general advice for any sloped lot.

If a basement is damp or a pump struggles, see sump pump repair. If a line slows in a house with an old drain, drain cleaning is the usual first step.

Water and sewer facts for all of Chelsea

Cold snaps matter more than water quality here, since MWRA water is soft at about 1 grain per gallon with a pH of 9 to 9.5. The sewer system is mostly combined, at about 70%, but that is a city figure and not a Powder Horn Hill finding.

The neighborhood to the northwest, Prattville, has its own page.

Powder Horn Hill plumbing questions

City planning documents use the name Soldiers Home for the area, and the hill is called Powder Horn Hill in the historical sources. We use both names for this page. The Soldiers Home itself is a state veterans' facility and not a home we serve here.

We have no evidence for that and we do not claim it. Slopes change where water goes, so we suggest checking downspouts and any sump pump.

A camera inspection of the main sewer line is a common step before a purchase. Call and we will tell you what it covers and what it does not.
