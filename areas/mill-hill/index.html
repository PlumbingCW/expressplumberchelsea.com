A small hill beside two creeks

Mill Hill is a mostly residential area of two- and three-story wood-frame detached buildings. It sits between Chelsea Creek and Mill Creek, on the Revere line, and Eastern Avenue runs across it. It is the smallest of the city's drumlins, at about 62 feet on the USGS figure.

The name comes from a tidal grist mill built on the creek by Thomas Pratt in 1734. The same site is now known as the Slade Spice Mill and includes Slade's Mill Apartments.

Low ground and the questions it raises

A building next to tidal creeks is close to the water table and to the places where stormwater collects. We do not claim that any Mill Hill home floods, and we have no flood data for it. We do say that every owner here should know where their sump pump discharges and whether the basement has a drain.

If you have a pump, test it by lifting the float, and see sump pump repair. If a basement is damp, basement waterproofing is a separate question from a pump and worth discussing on the phone.

Eastern Avenue shops and mixed buildings

The Massachusetts Historical Commission report notes that a commercial strip had formed along Eastern Avenue at Mill Hill by the mid-20th century. A shop or small business has different needs from a house, such as floor drains and shared bathrooms.

Owners of those spaces can read about commercial plumbing. The 1980 report is dated, so check what is on the street today.

A basement water check you can do today

This takes ten minutes and needs no tools.

  1. Look at the floor edgesCheck where the wall meets the slab for staining or a white crust.
  2. Find the drain and pumpNote whether there is a floor drain or a sump pit, and where the discharge pipe exits the house.
  3. Run the testPour a bucket of water into the pit and watch that the pump starts and the water leaves.
  4. Call if anything is offDescribe what you saw. We will say whether it needs a visit or can wait.

Water and sewer facts for all of Chelsea

Heavy rain can add load to a combined sewer system, and the city reports that about 70% of its sewers work that way. This is a citywide fact and not a Mill Hill finding. It is still a good reason to keep a sump pump working and a drain line clear.

Hardness is no worry, since the MWRA delivers soft water. The Historical Commission report describes workers' housing growing up the Mill Creek backslope toward Prattville, and Mill Hill borders Revere.
