Rebuilt after the 1908 fire

The Great Chelsea Fire of April 12, 1908 destroyed nearly half the city. The Historical Commission report says the burned area was rebuilt as a multiple-family district of three-deckers and apartments, and the center of the city was rebuilt around Bellingham Square, including City Hall.

The Downtown Chelsea Residential Historic District was listed on the National Register on June 22, 1988. It covers about 20 acres, roughly bounded by Shurtleff, Marginal and Division Streets and Bellingham Square. The 1909 Shurtleff School, which fills a whole block, helped spur housing here.

Many households under one roof line

City planning materials describe the Shurtleff Bellingham area as very high in population density, with clusters of small two- to four-unit buildings and many households of five or more people. More people on one set of pipes means more daily load on the same kitchen and bathroom drains.

When a first-floor sink and the unit above both back up, the shared line is the first suspect, and a drain cleaning visit with a camera can confirm it. We say this as a general rule of multi-unit buildings, not a finding about a street here.

Heavy storms can strain a combined sewer system, so keep a note of when a backup happens. A sewer camera inspection shows what is in the line.

Water and sewer facts for all of Chelsea
