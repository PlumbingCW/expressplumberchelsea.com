Plumbing on a hill with a naval hospital past

Admirals Hill sits on a point of land between the Mystic River and the Island End River. The Naval Hospital here was completed and commissioned on January 7, 1836, and it was decommissioned in 1974. The hill took the name Admirals Hill in the early 1980s, when the former hospital was converted to condominiums.

Today the area is mostly residential, and city planning materials list it among the lower-density parts of Chelsea with a high share of homeowners. A lot of plumbing questions here are condo questions: whose pipe is it, who pays, and who do I call first.

Condo plumbing: who owns what

In a condo, the association usually owns the shared risers and drains, and the unit owner owns the lines from the wall into the unit. The split depends on your master deed, so read it or ask your property manager before you book a repair.

A leak that reaches the unit below can bring a claim against the owner above, so speed matters. Shut the water to your unit, tell your manager and call us. A leak detection visit can show which side of the wall the water is coming from.

Calls we expect from condos and townhomes

Water heaters

A tank in a closet or utility room has little space around it. Planned replacement avoids a failure on a weekend.

Toilets and fixtures

Running toilets and dripping faucets waste water in every building. See toilet repair and faucet repair.

Slow drains

In a shared stack, a slow drain in one unit may be a building line. Say so when you call so we can plan the visit.

Hidden leaks

Check the ceiling below and the cabinet under each sink. Staining on a wall can come from a line inside it.

Landmarks around the hill

Mary O'Malley Park lies between the former hospital and the Mystic River shoreline and is described as the largest public park in Chelsea. The perimeter wall, chapel and Captain's House from the hospital era still stand.

We mention them only to help you place your address. We do not claim anything about the plumbing inside these historic buildings, and we look at each unit as it is.

Water and sewer facts for all of Chelsea

Water here comes from the MWRA, which keeps hardness near 1 grain per gallon and adjusts pH to between 9 and 9.5 to protect pipes. Separately, the city's sewer system is mostly combined, at about 70%, meaning one pipe carries both stormwater and household waste.

These are citywide facts, not findings about Admirals Hill. For a nearby view across the water, see our East Boston page.
