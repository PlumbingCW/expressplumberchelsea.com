Leak detection in Chelsea, MA is the process of finding where water is escaping from a pipe without tearing open walls and floors to guess. A hidden leak shows up as a water meter that keeps moving, a bill that jumps, a damp spot on a wall or ceiling, or a warm patch on a floor.

If water is actively spraying or pooling, shut the main valve first, then call our 24/7 line. If the problem is a slow mystery, start with the meter test below, because it tells you in ten minutes whether a leak exists at all.

Is it a leak? The meter test

Chelsea's Water and Sewer Department explains that a water meter has a low-flow indicator that turns whenever water passes through. With every fixture and appliance off, the indicator should be still.

If it keeps turning, water is moving somewhere. The usual suspects are a running toilet, a dripping faucet and the water heater, and when none of those explain it, the leak is in a hidden pipe and needs a trained ear and equipment to find.

Symptoms we hear most and where they point

A bill that doubles with no change in habits usually means water is escaping steadily, often from a buried service line or a slow slab-level leak. A wet ceiling below a bathroom points to a drain, supply line or shower pan above it.

Warm spots on a floor suggest a hot water line under the slab, and low pressure at every tap suggests a leak or a failing valve upstream. We match each symptom to the likely zone before we put a sensor on anything.

Emergency leaks in ceilings, walls and floors of older buildings

Chelsea's dense blocks of two- and three-story buildings stack bathrooms and kitchens over each other, so a leak in one unit shows up as a stain in the unit below. Sorting out which unit's fixture is responsible is the first job.

Basements and crawl areas in older buildings hide more. Damp framing near a supply pipe, a drain that weeps at the joint, or efflorescence on a foundation wall each tell a different story, and we work through them in order.

Buried service lines and the yard or meter box

The pipe that brings water from the street to your meter can leak where you cannot see it. Chelsea's lead service replacement program means many of these lines have been replaced already, but older private-side segments may remain.

A wet spot in a yard, a meter pit that fills with water or pressure that drops after the meter all point to this zone. If the service line itself has failed, water service line repair and replacement is the next step.

Irrigation, outdoor spigots and commercial buildings

A sprinkler zone or outdoor spigot can leak underground for weeks before anyone notices, because the water soaks away. A pressure test on the irrigation branch separates that leak from the house plumbing.

For shops, restaurants and multi-unit buildings, a leak adds to a larger shared bill and often to a lease dispute. We map the building by zone and document which tenant space the leak serves, which is useful when commercial plumbing repairs are involved.
