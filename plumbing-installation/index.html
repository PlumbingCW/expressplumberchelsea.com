Plumbing installation in Chelsea, MA covers adding or replacing fixtures such as sinks, toilets, tubs, showers, dishwashers and washing machine hookups, as well as new supply and drain lines for remodels and additions.

Massachusetts requires a permit for installing or altering plumbing, and only a licensed plumber can pull it. We handle the permit and the inspection so the work is documented and legal.

What we install

Our installation work includes kitchen and bathroom fixtures, shutoff valves, supply lines, washing machine and dishwasher connections, utility sinks, outdoor spigots and hose bibs, and new drain and vent runs.

For remodels, we rough in the plumbing before walls close and set the fixtures after finishes are done. For replacements, we confirm the old connections are sound before the new unit goes in.

Permits and inspections in Massachusetts

State law requires a plumbing permit for installing, replacing or altering plumbing and fixtures, and permits go to the local inspector through a licensed plumber. Homeowners may pull permits for their own single-family home in some cases, but they take responsibility for the work.

You can check any plumber's license on the state Division of Occupational Licensure website. Ask the city which local inspector reviews plumbing work for your address.

How an installation is run

  1. PlanWe review drawings or measure the space and confirm what the fixtures need.
  2. PermitA licensed plumber files for the permit and schedules inspections.
  3. InstallWe run lines, set fixtures and connect shutoffs.
  4. Test and inspectWe pressure test, check for leaks and meet the inspector.

Planning a remodel with the plumber early

The best time to talk to a plumber is before the cabinets, tile and flooring are ordered. Fixture locations, drain sizes and vent paths decide what is possible, and moving a drain after walls are closed is expensive.

We review plans, point out conflicts and tell you what the inspector will want to see. A short conversation early often saves a larger change later.

Water pressure, shutoff valves and future repairs

A new fixture is a good time to add a shutoff valve where there was none. Each fixture with its own valve means a repair later does not shut off water to the whole house. It is a small cost during installation.

We also check static pressure. Too high shortens the life of fixtures and appliances, and a pressure reducing valve is a simple fix.

Fitting new fixtures into older Chelsea buildings

Older buildings have drain lines of different sizes and materials, vents that are marginal for modern fixtures and shutoff valves that may not close. We check these before adding load, and replace them when needed.

In multi-unit buildings, a new bathroom may tie into a shared stack. We confirm capacity and coordinate with the building owner or association.
